## Adaptive secondary mirrors
I work on developing universal, facility-wide AO capability via adaptive secondary
mirrors (ASMs). The IRTF ASM project is the first on-sky demonstration of a new
actuator technology that enables more simple and robust ASMs. It serves as a pathfinder
for the Keck I ASM and retrofitting AO on existing instruments to drastically improve
sensitivity.

- **Lee, E.** et al., 2026, ["Facility integration of the NASA IRTF adaptive secondary mirror,"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00373), SPIE
- **Lee, E.** et al. 2026, ["Progress report on the integration of the IRTF adaptive secondary mirror,"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04385) AO4ELT8 <--- see below*
- **Lee, E.** et al., 2024, ["First laboratory and on-sky results of an adaptive secondary mirror with TNO-style actuators on the NASA Infrared Telescope Facility,"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06444), SPIE

I have demonstrated that IRTF-ASM-1 is robust and servicable in an observatory.
I have also implemented an enhanced seeng mode using IRTF's off-axis facility wavefront
sensor Felix. Depending on the conditions, this mode of operation can make the
background-limited sensitivity of IRTF comparable of that of a static 7- to 8-meter
telescope.[^1]

*Despite the inconspicuous name of this conference proceeding, it presents a number
of interesting results, including the first demonstration of retrofitting a seeing-limited
slit spectrograph with AO capability.

## Other work

Simulating image slicer integral field units in Zemax (ray tracing highly discontinuous,
regularly spaced surfaces). Many times faster than the traditional method of ray
tracing with multiple configurations.
- **Lee, E.,** 2026, ["Sequential and non-sequential Zemax Dynamic Link Libraries for generating image slicer integral field units,” ](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JATIS.12.2.025003) JATIS

Near-infrared line profiles of FU Orionis objects--mostly consistent with Keplerian
disk motion, although with unexplained asymmetry.
- **Lee, E.,** and Connelley, M., 2026, ["Probing the kinematics of FU Orionis objects through high-resolution near-infrared spectroscopy"](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae67f6), ApJ

'Commissioning a wide-angle (30' FOV) finder scope for photometric monitoring and
