## nice inversion layer
Low, flat clouds are a good sign.
![Layer of clouds below Maunakea](/atmosphere/clouds_maunakea-2.jpeg)
If you see layers of clouds all the way up to the summit, everything is stirring
together. The inversion layer is messed up.

# amorphous blobs of a different origin

So this isn't related to the atmosphere but I really like Cnidaria, which includes
jellyfish and [siphonophores](https://youtu.be/8KZsrDGLUJQ?si=eegq6_pvLf7lpPVu).

I visited the Monterey Bay Aquarium after a conference and saw their wonderful
jellyfish exhibits. I enjoyed staring at the [comb jellyfish](https://youtu.be/sTFskdKVNs4?si=TvsGduGLWO_S2mFW) a lot. It turns
out that they do not belong to the phylum of Cnidaria, but rather to their own Ctenophora.

I loved all of the comb jellyfish but I especially liked the deep sea ones that
looked like [fuzzy pickles](https://youtu.be/JwS2_5TPfUE?si=v0bGQcXkpLu3HQLL).
They shimmer spectacularly and are stunningly intricate in their movements,
like a mechanical pocket watch. I could watch them for a verrrry long time.
Time flies when you're having fun!
