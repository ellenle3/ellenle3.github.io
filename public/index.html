Adaptive secondary mirrors

I work on developing universal, facility-wide AO capability via adaptive secondary mirrors (ASMs). The IRTF ASM project is the first on-sky demonstration of a new actuator technology that enables more simple and robust ASMs. It serves as a pathfinder for the Keck I ASM and retrofitting AO on existing instruments to drastically improve sensitivity.

  • Lee, E. et al., 2026, “Facility integration of the NASA IRTF adaptive secondary mirror,”, SPIE
  • Lee, E. et al. 2026, “Progress report on the integration of the IRTF adaptive secondary mirror,” AO4ELT8 <— see below*
  • Lee, E. et al., 2024, “First laboratory and on-sky results of an adaptive secondary mirror with TNO-style actuators on the NASA Infrared Telescope Facility,”, SPIE

I have demonstrated that IRTF-ASM-1 is robust and servicable in an observatory. I have also implemented an enhanced seeng mode using IRTF’s off-axis facility wavefront sensor Felix. Depending on the conditions, this mode of operation can make the background-limited sensitivity of IRTF comparable of that of a static 7- to 8-meter telescope.1

*Despite the inconspicuous name of this conference proceeding, it presents a number of interesting results, including the first demonstration of retrofitting a seeing-limited slit spectrograph with AO capability.
