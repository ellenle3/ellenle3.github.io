Have you ever been in 70 mph winds at 14,000 ft above sea level? (Please say “no.”) It’s pretty dangerous, especially if there’s ice on the road. Parking your car in the same direction as the wind will make it difficult to shut the doors. It may also becomes unsafe to walk in exposed areas due to the risk of being catapulted off the mountain by a particularly strong gust of wind.

nice inversion layer

When the clouds are low and flat like this, it usually means the seeing is going to be decent.

If you see layers of clouds all the way up to the summit, the inversion layer is messed up because everything is stirring together. It looks interesting but is not a good sign for observers.

rainbow

Lots of rainbows in Hawai’i. This one was taken near the entrance of the Maunakea access road after a week-long observing run at the summit. It’s sooo small!! I wonder if there’s a pot of gold at the end of it.

Sunset comes with the bustle of tourists and a sense of anticipation for the long night of work ahead. Is it excitement? Dread? Or both? But after a long night at the telescope, I can watch the sunrise in complete silence. I also say goodbye to the observatories as I watch their domes close up. I feel relieved when I can go down to where there is more oxygen and moisture, but I also become a bit sad to leave.

amorphous blobs of a different origin

So this isn’t related to the atmosphere but I really like Cnidaria, which includes jellyfish and siphonophores.

I visited the Monterey Bay Aquarium after a conference and saw their wonderful jellyfish exhibits. I enjoyed staring at the comb jellyfish a lot. It turns out that they do not belong to the phylum of Cnidaria, but rather to their own Ctenophora.

I loved all of the comb jellyfish but I especially liked the deep sea ones that looked like fuzzy pickles. They shimmer spectacularly and are stunningly intricate in their movements, like a mechanical pocket watch. I could watch them for a verrrry long time. Time flies when you’re having fun! HAHAHA!
